AI tools for contractors: what's worth using, what's a waste.

Personal picks from a guy who has tested a lot of them. What actually saves time on a job site and what is burning your wallet for a feature demo. Covers voice-to-text proposals, ChatGPT for everyday writing, job documentation, review response tools, and when follow-up automation actually pays off.

BY NICK CONLEY FOUNDER · BLUE COLLAR TECHY
// The real question to ask

AI tools are being sold to contractors from every direction right now. YouTube pre-rolls, Instagram ads, email sequences, Facebook group posts. Someone is always trying to get you to pay $99 a month for a platform that will "transform how you do proposals."

I have been on both sides of that pitch. I ran a hardscape company for ten years. I know what a job site looks like at 6 AM and what a proposal looks like at 11 PM. Now I help contractors build their tech and marketing systems through Tekton Growth. I test a lot of tools. Most of them are not worth the monthly fee.

The question is not "should I use AI." For most contractor businesses running over $300K, the answer is yes, for specific things. The question is which of the forty tools being marketed at you are worth a slot in your stack and which ones to skip.

This post covers the tools I actually use or recommend to contractor clients. Real names, current prices, and one honest line about what they are actually good for. The last section covers the categories I think are mostly hype right now, so you know what to pass on.

No affiliate links in this post. No sponsored sections. If a tool is good, I say so. If it is a waste, I say that too.

// The tool that does the most for the least

Start here. ChatGPT is the most underused tool in the trades right now.

ChatGPT at $20 a month does 80% of what the fancy contractor-specific AI platforms charge $99 a month for. The difference is convenience, not capability. For most contractors, the convenience gap is not worth the price difference.

Here is what I use it for, and what I see working consistently for contractor clients:

  • Review responses. Paste the review in. Ask for a two-sentence reply that's warm and specific, not groveling. Thirty seconds per review instead of staring at a blank field for five minutes. Edit the output, but you're never starting from nothing.
  • Follow-up emails. Type the job details in plain English. Ask it to write a short, professional follow-up for a quote you sent three days ago. Done in under a minute.
  • Change order write-ups. Describe what changed on the job in your own words. Ask it to turn that into a clear scope description a homeowner will actually understand. No more hand-typed paragraphs at midnight.
  • Google Ads copy. Give it your service and city. Ask for ten headline options and five description variations. Pick the ones that sound like you. Saves a few hours when setting up a new campaign.
  • Proposal language. You know the scope. You know the materials. Getting the words on the page is the slow part. Use ChatGPT to draft the language, then tighten it up yourself.

One thing to know: ChatGPT does not know your trade. It will not understand the difference between a dry-laid paver base and a concrete-set one, or which drainage solution fits your soil type. It writes clear English. You supply the technical judgment. That combination works. Either one alone doesn't.

Use It ChatGPT Plus · $20/month

ChatGPT

The generalist that earns its place in any contractor's stack. Proposal language, follow-up emails, review responses, ad copy, change order write-ups. It writes. You edit. That split saves real time for any shop running over $500K. The free tier works for light use. Pay for Plus if you are going to rely on it daily.

Best use: anything where you know what you want to say but hate writing it.
// The workflow that saves the most time per bid

Voice notes into proposals. Stop typing estimates from scratch.

The most time-consuming part of running a contracting business is not the job. It is the paper around the job. Proposals, scope write-ups, project notes. Most contractors type these at night, from memory, in a hurry, after a full day on site.

There is a better way, and it does not require buying anything you probably don't already have on your phone.

Walk the job with your phone recording. Talk through everything out loud: square footage, grade, drainage concerns, material selections from the walkthrough, timeline, anything the homeowner said that changed the scope. A thorough site walkthrough takes three to five minutes. Do it while the details are fresh. You won't forget the thing you noticed at the corner of the house. You won't misremember the homeowner's deadline.

Then use a transcription app to turn that audio into text. Paste the text into ChatGPT with a prompt like: "Turn this job site walkthrough into a professional proposal scope section." You get a clean, organized first draft in about thirty seconds. Edit it for accuracy, add pricing, send it.

This workflow reliably saves 30 to 45 minutes per complex bid compared to starting from a blank page. On a week with five bids, that is three to four hours back.

Use It Otter.ai · Free up to 300 min/month · Pro $8.33/month billed annually

Otter.ai

Records and transcribes your voice in real time on your phone. Built for meetings, works fine for job site walkthroughs. The free tier covers 300 minutes a month, which is more than most contractors need. Record yourself walking the property, let it transcribe, copy the text, paste into ChatGPT to polish. Two free tools doing the work a $99 "AI proposal platform" claims to handle.

Best use: turning your own words from a site visit into a draft proposal scope section.

If your phone already has a decent voice memos app, that works for the recording. You're just adding a transcription step after. Otter handles both at once, which is why I mention it by name.

// If you're already in estimate software

AI in your estimate software. Turn it on if it's already there.

A handful of the contractor estimate apps have started adding AI features into their existing pricing tiers. Joist is one. If you are already paying for Joist, the AI-assist features are worth turning on. They suggest line items based on your trade, help structure estimates, and reduce the blank-page problem on routine bids.

The same principle applies to whatever platform you are already running. ServiceTitan, Jobber, and BuilderTrend have been rolling out AI features since late 2025. Before you sign up for a separate AI proposal tool, check whether your current software has added this capability. Most have. Check the feature release notes before adding another subscription.

Use It Joist · $8–$32/month depending on tier

Joist (with AI assist)

The easiest on-ramp for solo contractors who want AI-assisted proposals without a steep learning curve. If you are already using Joist, the AI features are low friction and worth enabling. If you are not in Joist, do not switch just for AI. Estimate software fundamentals matter more than any single feature.

Best use: contractors already in the Joist ecosystem who want a faster first-draft estimate on routine jobs.
// The photo habit that compounds

CompanyCam's AI Notes. The one AI feature that fits naturally into a crew workflow.

Most AI features sold to contractors require the crew to change how they work on site. Most of them don't stick. The crew is focused on the job. Anything that adds steps to their day gets dropped by week two.

CompanyCam is different because the core value is the photos themselves, and crews will take photos when it is easy. The app is GPS-tagged, organized by project, and accessible from the office or truck. That is the real product. The AI Notes feature sits on top of it and turns a set of job site photos into a written inspection summary in about thirty seconds. Scan the photos of the retaining wall, the drainage trench, or the mid-project patio and it generates a written summary of what the photos show.

The secondary payoff matters too. A crew taking consistent job site photos is also feeding your Google Business Profile photo queue and your website gallery. Photo freshness is a local SEO ranking signal. The habit that makes CompanyCam work is the same habit that builds your GBP over time. That double return is worth noting when you are deciding whether to add a new tool to the stack.

Use It CompanyCam · from $19/user/month · Core/Crew/Scale tiers

CompanyCam

Job site photo management with GPS tagging, project organization, and AI Notes that turn crew photos into written project summaries. The photo discipline is the hard part. If you can build that habit, the AI features are a free bonus on top of something that already helps your marketing and local SEO rankings. Start with one project and see if the crew actually uses it before committing the whole operation.

Best use: shops that document jobs for warranty, permit, or client communication and want those photos to earn their keep on the marketing side too.

// Reviews at volume

AI review response tools. Worth it when volume makes it a job.

If you are getting fewer than ten reviews a month, ChatGPT handles response drafts fine and you are already paying for it. If you are getting 30 to 50 new reviews a month and keeping up with responses has become a part-time job, a dedicated review response tool makes sense.

Reply Champion and RepliFast both auto-generate personalized Google review responses and can post them directly. They pull the reviewer's name, your business details, and the review text to generate something that reads like a person wrote it rather than a template. Both run at $10 to $12 a month.

The reason this matters in 2026 specifically: Google's local ranking algorithm now weights review response rate as a signal. A profile that responds to every review consistently ranks better than one that ignores them. If your response rate is below 80%, fixing that is one of the fastest free moves in local SEO. These tools make the habit easy to maintain at scale.

Use It Reply Champion · $10/month · RepliFast · $12/month

Reply Champion / RepliFast

Dedicated Google review response tools. Auto-generate personalized replies and post directly to your GBP. Worth the $10 to $12 a month if you are managing 30 or more reviews a month and they are sitting unanswered for days. Skip them if you are under ten reviews a month and can handle responses in the time it takes to paste into ChatGPT.

Best use: shops with high review volume who need consistent response without dedicating staff time to it.
// Follow-up at scale

GoHighLevel's AI. Real technology. Right fit at a specific size.

GoHighLevel has been expanding its AI features since 2025. The AI Employee add-on answers inbound calls, qualifies leads, and books appointments. The automated follow-up sequences handle the text and email touchpoints after a quote goes out. The CRM tracks which prospects responded and which went cold. It is a real system, not a feature demo.

The honest framing: this is a $97 a month base platform with a $97 a month AI add-on and real usage fees on top. Budget $200 to $250 a month once it is actually set up and running. It is worth it for shops doing $1M or more in revenue where slow follow-up is losing deals consistently. I have seen it perform well in those situations. I have also seen contractors at $350K sign up, spend six weeks trying to configure it, and cancel before getting a single automated sequence live.

The right size to look at GoHighLevel: you are running quotes consistently, you know your close rate, and you are losing jobs specifically because leads go cold between the estimate and the next contact. If that is your bottleneck, GHL is built to solve it. If your main problem is generating leads in the first place, address that before you automate the follow-up. Automating a leaky bucket is not the same as fixing the bucket.

Use It GoHighLevel · $97/month base + $97/month AI add-on + usage fees · Budget $200–$250/month

GoHighLevel

CRM with AI voice agent, automated multi-channel follow-up, lead tracking, and text and email sequences. The power tool of this list. Right for established shops at $1M-plus where slow follow-up is the documented constraint. Wrong fit under $500K, where the setup time and cost erase the ROI before the system is running.

Best use: contractors losing deals to slow response, not contractors still trying to generate leads in the first place.
// What I'd put back on the shelf

The tools that are mostly hype in 2026.

This is where a lot of the marketing budget aimed at contractors ends up. These categories sound compelling in an ad and don't deliver in practice.

  • AI "SEO" content platforms. The $99 lifetime tools that auto-generate 50 blog posts or city pages. I tested one of these for a full post on this site. The output had fabricated statistics, interchangeable city descriptions, and no real knowledge of any specific trade. Google has gotten better at identifying low-effort content at scale. A handful of specific, honest pages built from real knowledge outperforms a hundred AI-generated ones. Full breakdown: I tested the $99 AI SEO tool contractors are being sold.
  • AI website builders. Platforms that promise a professional contractor site in ten minutes. The output looks like every other AI-built contractor site and lacks the specific service pages, city pages, and structural decisions that actually drive organic rankings. Build the site right once rather than rebuilding it at month six when the template stops performing.
  • AI for Instagram and social posting. Auto-generated content that followers identify immediately as not authentic. The trades run on trust and local reputation. An Instagram feed that reads like it was written by nobody in particular signals to potential customers that nobody is really home. If you cannot post consistently yourself, hire a real person to manage it.
  • AI cold outreach. Mass email and DM sequences auto-generated to reach homeowners who did not ask to hear from you. Cold AI outreach is not how a hardscape, remodeling, or service trade business grows. Reputation grows referrals. Referrals grow the business. Cold AI prospecting adds noise and burns your domain reputation.

// Where to start

The honest starter stack.

If you are not using any AI tools right now, here is the simplest progression to follow without wasting money on a setup that will not get used.

  • Week one. Sign up for ChatGPT Plus at $20 a month. Write your next review response with it. Write the follow-up email for a quote that's been out three days. That is the whole learning curve. Use it daily for two weeks before adding anything else to the stack.
  • Week two. Try the voice-note-to-proposal workflow on one real bid. Record yourself walking the site. Transcribe with Otter.ai's free tier. Paste into ChatGPT. See how much time it saves. If it fits your rhythm, the habit sticks. If it doesn't, you have lost nothing.
  • Month two. If reviews are coming in at volume and manual responses are becoming a time drain, add a $10 review response tool. If your crew takes consistent photos on jobs, try CompanyCam on one project before committing the whole operation. If follow-up is specifically where deals die, that is when to look at GoHighLevel, not before.

That progression builds from the cheapest and easiest to the most expensive and most complex. It also builds from what works at any revenue level to what requires a real operation underneath it to pay off.

The biggest mistake I see with AI tools in the trades: paying for a platform there is no time to set up, getting no value out of it, and canceling at month three. The tool is usually not the problem. The timing was wrong, and the starting point was too complicated.

Compact the base first. Then lay the pavers. Then expand the patio.
