Troubleshoot

Why your business isn't showing up on Google Maps.

There are seven reasons this happens. Most are fixable in an afternoon. The problem is most contractors jump to fix four or five without checking whether fix one is the actual culprit. Work the ladder in order.

BY NICK CONLEY FOUNDER · BLUE COLLAR TECHY
// The setup

For most contractors, the map pack drives more calls than the website. Three spots. Most customers pick from those three. If your business isn't in those three, you're invisible to a large slice of the people searching for what you do.

The question I hear most: "Why isn't my business showing up?" Sometimes a contractor means they don't show up at all. Sometimes they mean they're at position eight instead of the top three. Both are problems. Different causes.

I've audited a lot of contractor profiles. The cause is almost always in one of seven places. They compound on each other. Fix six is mostly irrelevant when fix one is still broken.

Think of it like base prep before a paver install. Skip the compacted base and everything you lay on top eventually shifts. Work fix one first. If it's clean, move to fix two. Don't jump ahead.

Before you start: search your business name in Google. Find the map listing. You are about to run each of these checks on that live profile.

// The seven fixes
Fix 01 Start here — always

Your listing is not claimed or not verified.

About one in nine Google Business Profiles is still unclaimed. The business exists on Google's map data, shows up when people search, and nobody who works there can edit it. If that's your profile, nothing else on this list matters until you fix it.

How to check: search your business name on Google. If the listing shows "Own this business?" you don't own it. If you can log in and edit every field, you're clear. If your profile shows "Verification pending" or an unverified notice, your map pack visibility is limited.

A suspended profile is a separate problem and the worst outcome. If your profile was suspended, you won't appear in Maps at all. Suspensions happen for keyword stuffing in the business name, a fake or unverifiable address, or a flag from a competitor. The reinstatement process runs through Google's appeals tool. As of 2026, once you open the document upload form you have 60 minutes to submit your supporting files. The window closes after that and you have to reopen the appeal. Timeline to resolution is typically three to fourteen business days.

What clean looks like

You are logged in as an owner, you can edit every field, and the profile shows a verified badge. No pending verification notice. No suspension flag.

Fix 02 NAP consistency

Your name, address, and phone don't match everywhere.

Google cross-references your profile against your website, Facebook, Yelp, the BBB, HomeAdvisor, Angi, and hundreds of smaller directories. If "ABC Paving LLC" on your GBP shows up as "ABC Paving and Concrete" on Yelp, Google reads those as two different businesses. That inconsistency can work against your rankings.

Address format matters more than most people think. "123 Main St" versus "123 Main Street" versus "123 Main Street Suite A" are technically different strings to a machine. Pick a format. Use it identically everywhere.

One trap contractors fall into: stuffing keywords into the business name field. "Best Paving Contractor Phoenix Fast Affordable" instead of your legal business name is against Google's terms. Competitors can and do report it. Google clips the extras, and you lose trust in the process. Use your legal business name. Only your legal business name.

What clean looks like

Business name, address, and phone are identical across your GBP, your website's contact page, and every directory where you are listed. Same format. Same abbreviations. Same spelling.

Fix 03 Category selection

Your primary category is too broad or just wrong.

Your primary category is one of the strongest relevance signals you send Google. It tells the algorithm what searches to consider your profile for. "General Contractor" is too broad. Someone searching for a roofer, a flooring crew, and a paving company could all theoretically match a "General Contractor" listing. That's not specific enough to rank well for any of them.

Pick the most specific category that accurately describes the majority of your revenue. If you do driveways and parking lots, "Paving Contractor" beats "General Contractor." If you build retaining walls and outdoor living spaces, "Landscape Contractor" or "Hardscape Contractor" is a better fit than "Home Improvement Contractor." If your primary service is roofing, your primary category should say "Roofing Contractor."

Secondary categories expand your footprint. Add three to five that cover the other services you actually do. The right combination helps you show up for related searches you would otherwise miss entirely.

What clean looks like

One specific primary category that matches your main revenue source. Three to five secondary categories that map to real work you perform. No categories you don't actually offer.

Fix 04 Spam and duplicates

Competitor spam or your own duplicate listings.

Sometimes the issue is not what's wrong with your profile. Sometimes it's what's wrong with the map pack itself.

Keyword-stuffed business names are common in competitive trades markets. A competitor showing up as "Absolute Best Roofing Dallas Cheap Fast Licensed" instead of their actual business name is breaking Google's rules. Google relies on the public to report these. Search the map pack for your primary service. If you see listings with obvious keyword stuffing in the name field, use the "Suggest an edit" option on that listing to flag it. It doesn't resolve overnight, but it works.

The other kind of duplicate is your own. If you've created two profiles for the same business at the same or different addresses, those listings split your signals. Google may suppress both. If you find a duplicate of your own profile, use the "Claim this business" flow on the secondary one to merge or close it.

What clean looks like

One GBP for your business. No keyword-stuffed names in the top map results for your primary service. Any duplicate of your own listing has been merged or removed.

Fix 05 Proximity factors

You rank fine, just not where the searches are happening.

The map pack is geographically weighted. Google factors the distance between where a search is made and where your business is located. If your shop is on the east side of town, you'll rank better for searches made from the east side than from the west side of town. That's not a bug. It's how the algorithm works.

Service area businesses hit this constantly. A roofing company based in one suburb does half its work in the next suburb over and can't understand why they rank well at home and barely appear ten miles away. That's proximity working as intended. You need to understand it and work around it over time, not try to eliminate it.

What you can do: building authority in specific cities through location-based content, review clusters tied to those areas, and local citations helps expand your effective footprint. It doesn't happen in a week. And hiding your address doesn't escape proximity. Google still factors location data from your GBP even when the address isn't displayed publicly.

What clean looks like

Your service area is set accurately in GBP. You rank well in the geography closest to your address. Expanding that coverage into new areas is a months-long build, not an afternoon fix.

Fix 06 Competition strength

The top three in your market are just stronger right now.

Sometimes fixes one through five are clean and you're still not in the top three. That means the profiles outranking you are simply more established. Pull up the top two or three listings for your primary service and look at them honestly. How many reviews do they have? When was their last review? How long have they been active? How complete is their profile?

If the top result in your market has 280 reviews, a 4.9-star rating, posts every two weeks, and has been verified for six years, you are not going to out-rank them this week. That gap closes over time, not over a weekend.

The good news: you do not need to be first to get calls. Showing up at position two or three still puts you in the map pack. Position two still gets seen. The goal is the top three, not the top one. And the fastest path to the top three is consistently doing the things that compound: reviews, photos, posts, profile completeness. All of it. Month over month.

What clean looks like

You know the specific gap between your profile and the top three on reviews, recency, and completeness. You have a plan to close that gap steadily. Not in a sprint.

Fix 07 Review signals

Volume, recency, and response rate are all dragging you down.

Reviews affect map pack rankings in three specific ways: how many you have, how recently the newest ones came in, and whether you are responding to them.

Volume is the most visible gap. A business with 12 reviews and one with 180 reviews rank differently for the same search. That gap is not invisible to the algorithm and it is not invisible to the person choosing between listings.

Recency surprises people the most. A contractor with 150 five-star reviews stopped collecting new ones for six months. Rankings slipped. They restarted their review ask process, got ten new reviews over two weeks, and their map position bounced back within days. Google treats review freshness as a signal that the business is still active and still serving customers. An old stack of great reviews eventually looks like a business that used to be good.

Response rate is the third piece. Responding to reviews tells both Google and the next person reading your profile that someone is paying attention. You don't need a paragraph for each one. A sentence or two, acknowledging the specific job or the feedback, is enough. The point is that every review, including the negative ones, got a response.

What clean looks like

Review count is within striking range of the top competitor in your map pack. A new review has come in within the last 30 days. You have responded to every review, including the negative ones.

The order matters. Fix six is irrelevant when fix one is still broken.
The troubleshooting ladder
// Where to start

Run the ladder. Stop when you find the break.

Every one of these issues costs you calls. Some of them cost you a lot of calls. Most contractors I talk to are trying to fix five things at once and making no real progress on any of them.

Pick the first one you haven't confirmed is clean. Fix it this week. Then move to the next one.

  • Ownership and verification. If you don't control the profile, stop here and claim it. Everything else waits.
  • NAP consistency. Check your website, Yelp, Facebook, and the BBB. One hour of cleanup can close a leak that's been costing you for months.
  • Primary category. If you've changed your main service or grown past your original category, update it. This one takes five minutes.
  • Spam and duplicates. Scan the map pack for keyword-stuffed competitors. Report them. Check for duplicates of your own listing.
  • Proximity. Understand where you rank well and where you don't. Expanding your footprint into new areas is a long build, not a quick setting.
  • Competition gap. Know the specific gap. Build toward it. Consistently, not in bursts.
  • Review signals. Restart the ask process if it stalled. Respond to everything. Recency matters more than most contractors realize.
