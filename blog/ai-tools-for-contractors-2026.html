AI tools for contractors: what's worth using, what's a waste.

No affiliate links. No vendor relationships. Just the tools I actually use and the honest take on each one. Named. Priced. Worth it or not.

BY NICK CONLEY FOUNDER · BLUE COLLAR TECHY
// The honest picture

Most contractor AI content right now is written by people who have never run a crew. It's long on buzzwords and short on anything you could actually use on a job site. I've been on both sides. I ran a hardscape company for a decade before building the tech side. This post is what I actually use and what I've watched work for the contractors I work with at Tekton Growth.

Here's the real snapshot: only about 25% of residential contractors are actively using AI tools right now. Another 74% say they think it could help them. The gap between "think it could help" and "using it" is almost always a noise problem. Too many options. Too many vendors pitching too many things. Not enough of anyone saying "use this one, not that one, and here's why."

That's this post. Tools worth paying for, tools to skip, and the one tool that does most of the real work for $20 a month.

I'm not covering every AI product on the market. I'm covering the ones that come up in actual conversations with contractors or that I've run through myself. If it's not on this list, that's not a verdict. That's just scope.

// The $20 tool that does most of the work

ChatGPT Plus covers about 80% of what contractors actually need AI for.

ChatGPT Plus is $20 a month. That's it. No annual contract, no setup fee, no per-user pricing that balloons when you add your office manager.

The use cases where it actually delivers:

Voice notes to proposals

This is the one I see make the biggest difference the fastest. You walk a job. You talk through the scope into your phone. You've got a two-minute voice memo that covers materials, square footage, access constraints, and the customer's priorities. In the past, that memo sat in your pocket while you tried to remember all of it when you got back to the truck and wrote up the quote.

Now you transcribe it, drop it into ChatGPT with a prompt that matches your proposal format, and you get a structured first draft in about 30 seconds. You clean it up. You adjust the numbers. You send it. The proposal that used to take 25 minutes to write takes 8. Over a busy week that matters.

The prompt I use: "I'm a contractor. Here are my job notes from a site visit. Write a professional project proposal draft from these. Use plain language. Include a summary of scope, materials, timeline estimate, and a next steps section. Here are the notes: [paste]." That's the whole thing.

Quote follow-up drafts

You sent an estimate three days ago. No response. You need to follow up without sounding desperate or annoying. This is a small but real friction point for most owner-operators. ChatGPT handles it in 30 seconds. Give it the context, ask for a follow-up message that's professional and direct, and edit from there.

Same thing works for: estimate rejection responses (where you want to keep the door open), post-job thank-you messages with a review ask built in, and seasonal outreach to past customers.

Review response drafting

Responding to every Google review takes time when you're doing it from scratch. ChatGPT reduces the friction. Give it the review text, ask for a response that's personal, short, and doesn't sound like a template. Read it, edit the first name in, post it.

The Google reviews playbook covers the full response strategy. ChatGPT is just the drafting tool. You still need to read what it produces before you post it. Always.

Google Ads copy

If you're running Google Ads, AI drafts headlines and descriptions faster than writing from scratch. Give it your service, your city, and two or three things that make your company different. Ask for five headline and description variations in Google Ads format. Pick the ones that sound like you. You'll still need to tweak them, but the blank page problem disappears.

// Tools worth adding once ChatGPT is working

Two tools with clear, measurable ROI for smaller shops.

Jobber AI Receptionist ($99/mo add-on)

Jobber launched its AI Receptionist in August 2025. It answers calls and texts 24/7, qualifies the lead, and books an estimate on your calendar. The add-on sits on top of a Jobber subscription, so you're paying $99/mo on top of your base plan cost.

The case for this one is straightforward. Missed calls are expensive. HVAC contractors lose an estimated $45,600 a year to unanswered calls. For plumbers the number runs higher. If you're a one-truck or two-truck operation and you're on a job when the phone rings, every unanswered call is a potential estimate that went to your competitor instead. The AI Receptionist catches those calls around the clock without you hiring a part-time person to sit at a desk.

This only makes sense if you're already on Jobber. If you're on a different platform, check whether your CRM has a comparable feature before paying for a second tool.

NiceJob ($75/mo)

NiceJob handles review requests automatically. A job closes in your CRM, a text goes out to the customer with your Google review link, a follow-up goes three days later if no review comes in. That sequence, run consistently after every job, is what the contractors with 200-plus reviews actually did. Most of them did not run a special campaign. They just had a consistent ask process running in the background.

NiceJob is the budget option that's month-to-month and doesn't require an annual contract. It also drafts AI responses to reviews, which you review and post. Clean, simple, and does exactly what it says.

The bigger reputation platforms (Birdeye at $299/mo, Broadly at $399/mo for the full suite) make sense for multi-location businesses managing review volume across dozens of listings. For a single-trade, single-location contractor, NiceJob is almost always the right fit at a fraction of the cost.

Quick-reference pricing sheet

What the tools actually cost

  • ChatGPT Plus: $20/mo. Proposals, copy, review response drafting, follow-ups. Start here.
  • Jobber AI Receptionist: $99/mo add-on (requires Jobber subscription). 24/7 call and text answering, estimate booking. Best fit for operators who miss calls on the job.
  • NiceJob: $75/mo, month-to-month. Automated review request sequences, AI response drafts. Best fit for contractors who need a consistent review process without a platform commitment.

// What to skip

Tools that are real but priced wrong for most of you.

Rilla ($4,000+ per user per year)

Rilla is an AI tool that records in-field sales conversations and gives sales reps structured coaching feedback. The idea is sound. The pricing is not built for a three-truck operation. You're looking at $4,000 or more per user per year, a five-user minimum, and a $1,500 to $5,000 setup fee. That's $20,000 or more just to get started.

Rilla makes sense for a regional HVAC company running 20-plus service techs who need consistent sales process across a large team. It does not make sense for an owner-operator closing estimates personally. Save the budget.

ServiceTitan (pricing is quote-only, typically $3,000 to $10,000/mo total)

ServiceTitan is a serious platform with serious AI features. It's also built for serious-sized companies. Most of the contractors I talk to who signed a ServiceTitan contract at $1 to $3M in revenue describe the same experience: too many features, too steep a learning curve, and a price tag that doesn't fit until you're substantially larger.

If you're running a lean crew under $3M, Jobber or Housecall Pro fit better and cost less. The AI features inside ServiceTitan are impressive. So is the monthly bill. Get the foundation right at the right price point for your size first, then scale the platform when the revenue supports it.

AI content tools marketed as SEO solutions

I wrote about this directly in a separate post. The short version: tools that promise to write your SEO content automatically are producing thin, generic articles that don't reflect your trade, your region, or your actual knowledge. Google has gotten better at identifying that content at scale. A few specific, well-written pages beat a hundred generated ones every time.

Use ChatGPT to help you write. Read what it produces. Edit it to sound like you and to reflect what you actually know about your work. The final page should reflect your real knowledge about real work in a real place. If it could be about any contractor in any city, it probably won't rank for yours.

// What AI can't do for you

The part most AI content skips.

AI is a drafting tool. It is not a substitute for showing up, doing good work, or asking your customers for reviews when the job is done. That part is still on you.

The adoption gap is real. Only 25% of residential contractors are actively using these tools, despite three in four saying they believe AI could help. The ones getting traction are not buying every new tool that launches. They're using one or two things consistently after every job.

ChatGPT Plus running after every estimate. NiceJob sending a review request after every closed job. That's it. Not ten tools, not a new app every quarter. Consistency beats novelty every time in this business.

The other thing AI doesn't solve: the underlying business. If your close rate on estimates is low, a better proposal template helps at the margin. But the real problem is probably something in the conversation before the proposal. If your review count is low, an automated follow-up helps. But if the job experience was bad, no follow-up fixes that.

The 10 things no marketing agency can fix covers the owner-side fundamentals that no tool, AI or otherwise, can patch. Read that one alongside this one. The tools amplify what's working. They don't create it.

Where to start

If you're not using AI at all today, start with ChatGPT Plus at $20 a month. Use it for your next three proposals. See how much time it saves. See whether the output sounds close enough to your voice that editing is fast. That experiment costs you one month's subscription and a few hours. The verdict will be obvious either way.

If proposals aren't your pain point, use it for review responses first. Or follow-up emails. The tool is flexible enough to test in whichever area feels most tedious before you commit to a workflow around it.

After 30 days of actually using it, you'll have a much clearer picture of where it fits in your specific business than any post on the internet can tell you. Including this one.
